Team 11 – Cookie Shop

Written by: Jonas (jonashey1), on behalf of Team 11.

This agreement explains how our team works together on our cookie ordering website. Every member has read and agreed to these rules. We review the agreement when necessary.

How we share the roles: We follow the course's default for a team of six. Each member has one main role. Jonas and Xander work together on the repository, releases and content model. Tessa and Esma work together on the informational task and design. Arnaud and Yana work together on the transactional task and testing. Everyone contributes to the code and documentation, even outside their main role.

## How we communicate

- Where we talk: We communicate through our Messenger group chat. We also discuss project-related matters in person when possible.
- How quickly we answer a teammate: We try to respond to messages within one working day. If someone is unavailable, they inform the group.
- When and how long we meet: We plan a short meeting of approximately 30 minutes every week, either in person or online, depending on everyone's availability.
- Where we write down what we agreed: We summarize important decisions and agreements in our Messenger group. Important project decisions are also documented in our GitHub repository.

## How we decide

- What a role holder decides alone: Each member can make small decisions within their own role, as long as these do not significantly affect other parts of the website. Important changes are communicated to the team.
- What needs the pair: Changes to a user task or its design are discussed within the responsible pair. Changes to the content model and database are coordinated between Jonas and Xander.
- What needs the whole team: Major changes to the website's purpose, requirements, functionality, design or planning must be discussed with the whole team.
- How we settle a disagreement, and when we write a decision record: We first discuss the different options in our Messenger group or during a meeting. We try to reach a shared decision. If we cannot agree, we decide by majority vote. Important technical decisions are documented with their reasons in the project's decision records.

## When a pull request is done

The author of a pull request merges it when all of this is true:

- The author tried the change on its preview, at a narrow width too when something visible changed.
- Its AI note is filled in.
- The starter's checks have finished. The author read what they say, and fixed what a red check found when that was possible.
- A change to the content model has its migration. Only the content-model pair creates migrations.

We ask a teammate for a review when the author is not sure of a change.

We also follow these additional rules:

- We use clear commit messages that describe the changes.
- We check that new functionality works correctly before merging.
- We make sure that changes do not break existing functionality.
- We inform the team through Messenger when important changes are made.
- We test the cookie ordering process before merging changes that affect orders.
- We only merge into `main` after checking the preview and reviewing the results of the automated tests.

## The AI note

Every pull request has the section AI note: what I asked AI, and what I checked, under that exact heading. The note is one or two lines and has two parts: What I asked AI and What I checked. When no AI tool was used, the note says "No AI used".

- How we check what an AI tool wrote before we use it: We review AI-generated suggestions and compare them with the course instructions and our existing code. We make sure we understand the suggested changes before using them. We test the results ourselves in VS Code and on the website. We only accept changes that work correctly and describe our actual AI use and verification in the pull request.

## When someone stops contributing

- What counts as stopping for us: A member does not respond to messages in our Messenger group for three working days without explanation, or repeatedly misses agreed deadlines without informing the team.
- What the pair does first: The pair partner contacts the member directly through Messenger to ask whether there is a problem and whether help is needed.
- What the whole team does next: If the situation does not improve, the team discusses it together. We redistribute tasks if necessary and agree on new responsibilities and realistic deadlines.
- How we record what happened: We change this agreement when we share the roles or the work again.

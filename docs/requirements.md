# Requirements

Team 11 – Cookie Shop

Written by: Jonas (jonashey1), on behalf of Team 11.

These requirements describe what our Cookie Shop website must do. They are based on the two user tasks in our project brief: ordering cookies (transactional) and finding and comparing cookies (informational). They also cover accessibility, privacy and performance.

| Id | Requirement | Serves | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | A customer can select one or more available cookies, choose the quantity of each and submit an order using made-up contact details. | Primary transactional task: customers need to order cookies. We will validate this need during usability testing before Hand-in 2. | On the live site, open the cookie assortment, select two different cookies, choose a quantity for each and submit an order with made-up details. The site must accept the order and show a confirmation that it was saved. | passed - Production test on 23 Nov 2026: order with 2 Classic Chocolate Chip and 3 Double Chocolate cookies submitted successfully. |
| R2 | A shop administrator can view every successfully submitted test order, including the selected cookies and quantities. | Primary transactional task: the shop needs accurate order information to organise preparation. We will verify this with test orders. | On the live site, submit a test order for two different cookies. Open /admin and find the order. Both selected cookies and their quantities must match the submitted order. | passed - Production Payload CMS check on 23 Nov 2026: both products and their exact quantities were correctly stored. |
| R3 | A customer can find the available cookies and understand the flavour, description and price of each product before ordering. | Informational task: customers need enough information to choose cookies. Inspired by the product presentation of Het Koekemannetje; we will validate this during user testing. | Open the cookie assortment on the live site. Every available cookie must have a name, description, flavour information and price that can be read without submitting an order. | passed - Production test on 23 Nov 2026: all six cookies displayed their name, flavour, description and price. |
| R4 | A customer can compare at least two available cookies using their product information before deciding what to order. | Informational task: customers want to compare flavours and prices. Their names, descriptions and prices must be accessible so the user can compare them. | passed - Production check on 25 Nov 2026 (commit edeac49): /cookies shows all six cookies with name, description and price, and its comparison section (#compare-cookies) puts two or three chosen cookies side by side. | R5 | A customer can complete the main ordering task using only a keyboard, without needing a mouse. | Accessibility: customers who cannot use a mouse must still be able to order. Every stop showed a visible focus outline. The final Enter was not pressed, to avoid an extra production order; the same server action is covered by R1. Done by a browser agent, not a person with assistive technology. |

## PR #27 closure evidence

The informational path begins at `/cookies`, with Comparison now a section on that page. The transactional path uses the same current CMS prices as the cards, and preserves the submitted order as a stored snapshot. Check the tests in `tests/e2e/final-ux.spec.ts` for guest write rejection, an authorised price edit, matching card and basket prices, price-change error and retry without a duplicate. A missing catalogue is treated as temporary unavailability, never as an alternate price list. The live evidence from the older production release remains historical.
